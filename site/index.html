Ambient covariate sensing · reproducibility

Measuring the room so experiments reproduce.

When an experiment won't replicate, the cause is often the room, not the protocol — a door left open, the HVAC cycling, a shift in temperature or light that no one wrote down. Covariate turns a commodity smartphone into a passive recorder of those invisible variables, then tests a sharp claim: that logging them is enough to explain — and correct — why the same experiment gives different answers in different places.

The problem

Reproducibility failures are often unlogged environmental variance.

Over 70% of surveyed researchers have failed to reproduce another's experiment, and more than half their own. When three labs ran identical mouse protocols with apparatus rigorously matched, they still saw systematic, lab-specific differences — evidence that conditions no protocol records can change an outcome. Those conditions are cheap to measure. Almost no one measures them.

The approach

Five sensors, one clock, tied to a specific experiment.

The phone logs the ambient context passively while your experiment runs, and exports a timestamped record you can attach to a result. It is built to be trustworthy enough to be metadata.

Barometer

Air pressure & relative altitude — weather fronts, HVAC, a door opening.

Accelerometer

Bench vibration — foot traffic, a centrifuge next door, a bump.

Magnetometer

Magnetic field — motors and appliances switching on.

Light

Ambient brightness — lights toggled, daylight, someone walking past.

Sound level

Loudness only — never audio. An RMS number, computed and discarded in place.

External (optional)

An ESP32 + BME280 add-on for temperature & humidity the phone can't sense.

Privacy by construction. The microphone yields a single loudness number and nothing that could contain speech. The camera reads only a brightness value; no photos or video are stored.

The study

Covariate-adjusted reproducibility, with a known-physics anchor.

We run a cheap, standardized assay — effervescent-tablet dissolution time, whose temperature dependence is physically known (rate roughly doubles per 10 °C) — across kitchens, times, and controlled-vs-disturbed conditions, logging ambient context throughout. The known physics is our internal check: the phone's covariate should predict the outcome shift the way theory says it must.

H1
Context sensitivity.

A disturbed run's sensor activity exceeds the same phone's controlled-run 95th-percentile baseline — tested one-sided, per device.

H2
Cross-device agreement.

Two phones watching the same event agree at Pearson r ≥ 0.9, with bias inside the channel's noise floor. A log two phones disagree about is worse than none.

H3
Cross-site reproducibility.

Across sites, raw outcomes disagree — and the disagreement shrinks once the logged covariate is included. Reproducibility, recovered from the ambient record.

Pre-registered before data collection. Instrument validation (H2) comes first: we don't claim the covariate explains variance until we've shown the phone measures it reliably. Negative results count — any channel that fails H2 is flagged untrustworthy, not dropped.

Get the app

Run the recorder on your phone.

The beta runs today through Expo Go — no install friction, no developer account.

  1. Install Expo Go (free) from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Open it and scan the project QR we share with you.
  3. The recorder loads on your phone — grant motion & mic permissions and hit record.

Current beta reads accelerometer, magnetometer, barometer, and sound level. The camera-light channel, the external BME280 module, and a standalone installable build are on the way. Android users will get a direct APK download here.
